A reproducible machine-learning study for prioritizing existing content pages for refresh review using observable search and content-performance signals.

Machine Learning Search Intelligence 30,000 pages Client-holdout validation Decision support

Abstract

This project asks whether observable content and search-performance signals can be used to prioritize pages that may benefit from refresh review. Using an anonymized dataset of 30,000 content-page records, I built a reproducible content-refresh prioritization pipeline covering feature preparation, leakage checks, baseline scoring, model training, validation, and ranked recommendations.

Three classification models were compared with a transparent rule-based baseline. Random Forest was selected using ranking-oriented evaluation and achieved 0.74 Precision@50, compared with 0.24 for the baseline on the same evaluation design. The resulting queue is intended as a human-review prioritization mechanism rather than proof that refreshing content will cause higher rankings, traffic, or revenue.

Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams can have thousands of existing pages but limited capacity to review them. A useful prioritization system should identify pages where the available evidence suggests that review may be worthwhile, while keeping the final editorial decision with a human reviewer.

The research question is: Can observable content and search-performance signals be used to prioritize pages that may benefit from refresh review?

The decision supported by this work is therefore operational: which existing content pages should a team investigate first?

Important framing: The model identifies patterns associated with the defined outcome in the dataset. It does not establish a causal relationship between refreshing a page and improved search performance.

Feature groups

The feature vector uses observable content and search-performance information, including search volume, competition, CPC, content type, intent, content size, content age, update recency, impressions, clicks, sessions, CTR, average position, engagement, scroll behaviour, AI traffic percentage, and appropriate historical trend signals.

Identifiers are retained only as context for grouping and validation and are not used as predictive evidence. Private queries, client names, URLs, domains and other identifying information are excluded from the public analysis.

Missing data

Missingness was explicitly inspected. Examples include approximately 25.66% missingness for word and character counts, 8.23% for search volume, CPC and competition, 7.91% for main intent, and 0.42% for scroll rate. The feature engineering pipeline handles missing values rather than silently dropping records.

Methodology

Problem formulation

The analysis treats content-refresh prioritization as a supervised binary classification and ranking problem. The target is whether a content item belongs to the defined declining/review-priority class.

Feature vector

The feature vector combines numerical search-performance and content signals with categorical content attributes. Numerical missing values are handled during feature preparation and categorical values are encoded for modeling. Skewed count variables are transformed where appropriate to reduce the effect of heavy tails.

Models

Three candidate models were compared:

  • Logistic Regression
  • Decision Tree
  • Random Forest

A transparent rule-based baseline was also constructed so that learned models could be evaluated against a simple, interpretable heuristic.

Validation

Validation uses a client-holdout split. This avoids randomly mixing records associated with the same client between training and evaluation, providing a stricter test of generalization across represented clients.

Leakage checks

The workflow checks for label-derived variables, future-window information, product flags, identifiers, and private query information. Information that would only become available after the prediction decision is not treated as predictive evidence.

Ranking

Model probabilities are converted into a ranked review queue. Ranking metrics, especially Precision@50, are emphasized because the operational use case is selecting a relatively small group of pages for human review.

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The model identifies observable patterns associated with the defined refresh-review label; it does not establish that refreshing content will cause higher rankings, traffic, clicks, or revenue.
  • Client-holdout validation helps evaluate generalization across represented clients, but it is not equivalent to a random experiment.
  • Feature importance describes model behaviour and should not be interpreted as causal importance.
  • The dataset contains anonymized and aggregated signals, so results may not generalize to other datasets or future search conditions.
  • Pages with missing or sparse observations may receive less reliable predictions.
  • The ranked queue is a prioritization aid. Human reviewers should consider content quality, search intent, accuracy, freshness and business context.
  • The model should not automatically publish, rewrite, delete or materially change content without human review.
  • Evaluation metrics describe the observed evaluation sample and are not guaranteed production performance.

Ranked Recommendations

The ranked refresh queue is the primary decision-support output. Pages near the top should receive earlier human review because they have stronger model evidence associated with the defined outcome.

  1. Review high-priority pages first. Investigate the strongest model-ranked signals before allocating broader content resources.
  2. Inspect reason codes. Review signals such as declining demand, CTR opportunity, engagement, position, content age and trend direction.
  3. Check content quality and intent. A high model score does not automatically mean that a page should be refreshed. Confirm that the page still satisfies its intended search intent.
  4. Choose the final action after human review. Possible treatments include:
    • Protect — no refresh required; continue monitoring.
    • Improve — targeted content, structure or metadata improvements.
    • Rewrite — substantial content changes where justified.
    • Merge — consider consolidation when pages substantially overlap.
    • Prune — consider removal only after separate quality and intent review.
    • Monitor — insufficient evidence for immediate action.
  5. Record the final decision. Keep model recommendations separate from the final human decision so future analysis can distinguish model ranking from reviewer judgement.
Production principle: The queue tells a team where attention may be most useful first. It does not make the final content-management decision.

Reproducibility

The work is organized as a reproducible repository-based workflow. The notebooks cover the research question, ML task framing, data contract, feature/leakage checks, baseline scoring, signal auditing, model training, validation auditing, action recommendations and the final capstone.

The reference pipeline prepares the feature vector, builds the baseline, trains the candidate models, evaluates the ranked queue, generates charts, writes the Markdown model report and produces the PDF summary.

The final pipeline successfully processed and scored all 30,000 records and selected Random Forest as the best model under the evaluation criterion.

The repository contains the generated model report and chart artifacts used to support this paper.
